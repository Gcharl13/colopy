Colony Calculus

Colony design in Colonization is a well-posed mathematics problem in physical flows — units of sugar, rum, muskets, bells and food per turn. Every coefficient below is taken from the reconstructed engine — the terrain tables the game ships in NAMES.TXT and the arithmetic byte-traced in VICEROY.EXE. From those rates this page derives a set of colony configurations, each maximizing one flow, then composes them into a supply network and instantiates them on the real AMER2.MP tile data. No market prices appear anywhere: production only.

Two ground rules for reading this. First, trust labels: values marked with a byte offset like @0x9DD2 are read out of the executable; values marked (R) come from the manual or the classic understanding of the game and are used only where the trace is incomplete. Second, prime resources (Beaver, Prime Sugar…) turn out not to be stored in the map file — the two trailer layers of AMER2.MP decode as region ids, so placement is runtime logic that is still TBD. The model therefore scores sites without resources; the byte-verified bonus table in §1 tells you exactly how much a found resource adds.

1The tile production function

One colonist working one tile produces one good. The engine (compute_terrain_yield, file 0x9B9C..0x9FFB) computes the amount as a short pipeline. Writing τ for the tile's terrain id, p for the profession column, e ∈ {0,1} for “the worker is the matching expert,” and I for the improvement state (cleared / plowed / road):

y(τ,p,I,e) = gate( (( T[τ][p] + nudge + tory ) ⊕ expert) + R(r,p)·(1+e) + improv(I,p) ) T[τ][p] base table, NAMES.TXT $TERRAIN, loaded at DGROUP:0x2F7B @0x9C1E ids 16..23 fold to 8..15 via (id&7)|8 @0x6225 nudge water column only: +1, or −1 / −2 with 6 / 8 water neighbours @0x9C3E tory −⌊ P·(1−SoL)/(10−difficulty) ⌋, PLUS +1 at rebel majority @0x9D49..0x9D98 and +1 at 100% (added again after the expert +2 on food cols) @0x9D88/@0x9DC3 ⊕ expert food columns (farmer, fisherman): +2 all others: ×2 @0x9DBF / @0x9DD2 R(r,p) resource bonus table (below); doubled for the expert @0x9AAA / @0x9E0A improv plow → crop columns, road → ore/fur/timber columns; bonus 1, or 2 on the lumber column or a river tile @0x9EC6..0x9F4C gate manufactured goods need their building; clamp ≥ 0 @0x9F4F..0x9F83

2Colony balance laws

Food. Each turn the colony eats exactly 2·P @0xA5F2. Sustained operation therefore needs F(x) ≥ 2P; anything above that accumulates toward the next colonist.

Liberty bells. Sons-of-Liberty membership is a ratio of two exponentially decayed accumulators, updated every turn @0x2DA1C..0x2DAD8:

B ← B − B/64 + 2P divisor: decay 1/64, inflow 2 per colonist A ← A − A/64 + b dividend: decay 1/64, inflow b = bells made this turn SoL = A/B fixed points: B* = 128·P A* = 64·b ⇒ SoL% = 50 · b / P ⇒ 100% needs bells ≥ 2 per colonist per turn

Building workers — the per-colonist producer func_009FFC, previously a SKELETON in the function inventory, decodes fully (traced 2026-08-15, rev 2 of this page):

rate = base + sol base: 3 free/expert · 2 servant · 1 criminal/convert @0xA0D7..A0F8 sol: +1 rebel-majority latch, +1 rebel-unanimous latch @0xA098..A0AC if shop tier: rate += base chain count > 1 @0xA1A4 if factory tier: rate += rate/2 chain count > 2 @0xA1B2 if expert: rate ×= 2 doubling comes LAST @0xA127/@0xA12C raw input = output × 2/3 at factory tier, else 1:1 func_008E84 @0x8EB1

So one expert in a factory at 100% SoL produces 24 finished from 16 raw (18 from 12 below rebel majority), and a full three-expert factory moves 72 out / 48 in per turn. The special producers use the same skeleton: an Elder Statesman makes (3+2)×2 = 10 bells; a Master Carpenter makes (6+2)×2 = 16 hammers with a Lumber Mill (the mill ×2 is building id 0x24 tested at @0xA11C — now byte-verified, no longer folklore); a Firebrand Preacher under a Cathedral makes 16 crosses. Colony bell total is then 1 + 10s (base +1 @0xA4DB) × Newspaper 2 or Printing Press 1.5 @0xA587..0xA5AC, Founding-Father op 0xF +50% @0xA4DF. The tory production penalty in §1 scales with population — a big colony below 100% SoL taxes every worked tile, which is the mathematical reason under-belled sprawl is expensive.

Manufacturing chains (func_008E84): Sugar→Rum, Tobacco→Cigars, Cotton→Cloth, Furs→Coats, Ore→Tools, and Tools→Muskets via the Arsenal chain. Output is capped by the workers' rate above; when raw runs short, output backscales to 1.5×raw at factory tier @0x8EF3..0x8EFA. Surplus goods auto-sell in Europe each turn @0x2D6F7..0x2D785, so steady-state value is a flow, not a stockpile (warehouse cap (level+1)·100, func_008D00, never binds a balanced colony).

3The configurations

A configuration is a colony template that maximizes one flow, found by solving, per template, the integer program

maximize flow[target] a single physical flow — no prices subject to Σₖ x[j,k] ≤ 1 one worker per ring tile Σ x + s + c + Σ m = P headcount F(x) + F₀ ≥ 2P + imports food law (§2); importer roles relax this 2·(1 + 10s) ≥ 2P own bells ≥ 2P — SoL is per-colony, never imported out[g] = 24·m[g], in[g] = 16·m[g] factory crews (§2); ≤3 workers per shop (R)

Standing assumptions: matching experts, 100% SoL, improvements complete, Newspaper in every colony (its min_colony is 4). Nominal tile rates from §1 at these settings: planter 11/tile (12 on river), farmer 10–11, ore miner 9–10, lumberjack 12. The catalog, in flows per turn:

Three structural facts shape the catalog. Bells are local: the SoL divisor inflow is 2·pop of that colony @0x2DA64, so every configuration carries its own statesman — there is no exporting liberty. Hammers are local: they accrue to the colony's own build bank @0x2E50F; the hammer phase is temporal, not spatial. Horses are stock-driven: growth is 2·⌈herd/25⌉ with a Stable, capped by half the food surplus and warehouse room @0xA5B0..0xA63F, so a ranch's output depends on the herd it keeps, not on workers.

The food books balance internally: suppliers throw off +87, importers draw 44 — no dedicated granary needed until the herd count or immigration grows. Per colonist, the cluster yields ≈3.8 finished sellable units per turn plus the war flow.

Revolution arithmetic. Equipping a unit costs 50 muskets; mounting it costs 50 horses (R). One cluster arms 1.4 regiments/turn but mounts only 0.48/turn — the Stable's stock-growth law makes horses, not muskets, the military bottleneck. Each additional ranch (+24/turn) closes the gap; three ranches match the armory's musket flow for dragoon production.

Movement arithmetic. Terrain movement costs are game data (NAMES $TERRAIN column: open land 1 MP, forest/marsh 2–3); a wagon train has 2 MP and a road cuts any tile to ⅓ MP (R) — so a roaded wagon covers 6 tiles/turn (diagonals count the same on the 8-way grid), against 1–2 tiles/turn cross-country. Roads triple-to-sextuple every edge's throughput, which is why the network below is drawn along its road spine.

The wagon budget is the binding constraint. Three rules govern the fleet (player-confirmed; not yet byte-traced): you may keep at most colonies − 1 wagon trains, each carries 2 holds of a single good, 100 units each, and its round-trip throughput is 200 ÷ (2·one-way turns) — 100/turn on a 1-turn leg, 50/turn on a 2-turn leg. Three design consequences follow. Ship the need, not the harvest: a factory eats 48/turn, so a spoke sends 48 and banks its 18-unit surplus locally — hauling the full 66 would double the fleet for nothing. Put the hub on the coast: ships are outside the wagon cap, so the export leg must never spend wagons — a pure-importer hub works zero land tiles, which also lets it legally sit adjacent to a spoke's ring without stealing tiles. Every new colony funds its own logistics: +1 colony = +1 wagon, so expansion and transport capacity grow in lock-step.

The 2-turn Furs and Ore legs are the tight ones: one wagon moves exactly 50/turn there, against a 48/turn factory appetite — a 2-unit margin. The spokes' 18-unit/good surpluses stay banked at the outposts until the next colony founding adds a wagon to lift them. Hub sits 2 tiles from the tobacco ring — legal and free of conflict, because an all-indoor hub works no land tiles.

Food from the sea — measured, not assumed. The engine's numbers at 100% SoL, expert worker: a coastal ocean tile fishes 10 (base 3 + adjacency +1 @0x9C3E + latches + era bonus); a plowed Plains farm grows 11–12. Deep ocean — 6/8 water neighbours — drops to 8/7. So tile-for-tile the sea is slightly weaker than the best land… with three offsets that still make coastal food the right buy: an ocean tile needs no pioneer work (a plow is 20 tools + turns), it has zero opportunity cost (water grows no cotton — every fish is a land tile freed for cash crops), and a Fishery resource is the best food tile in the game at 16 (+3 doubled for the expert, @0x9B86). A site's food ceiling is therefore driven by its land/sea split: mixed-coast rings farm the land and fish the water, and the harbor town doubles as the cluster's second granary.

7Build-out order

Two dynamics govern the path to the steady state above. Below 100% SoL the tory term punishes size: the per-tile penalty −⌊P(1−SoL)/(10−d)⌋ is superlinear in colony population (P tiles each lose ⌊P·…⌋), while a pop ≤7 outpost floors it to zero entirely — so found the spokes small and early, and concentrate statesmen where population concentrates. Hammers front-load: a two-carpenter yard (32 hammers/turn at 100% SoL) raises a shop (52–64 hammers) in two turns and a factory (160–240 + tools) in five to eight; every colony passes through the hammer phase before settling into its configuration, consuming its own forest ring as it clears it — the clearing lumber itself banks into the colony @0x04084D.
